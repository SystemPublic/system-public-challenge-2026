# Evidence synthesis decision — 14 November 2026

## What this pass establishes
This is a single AI-assisted screening and targeted extraction pass, not a duplicate-screened systematic review. All 87 original job-security/working-hours candidate publications received a title/abstract screen. Ten publications received targeted methods/results checks; 24 estimates from ten publications are in the extraction ledger (20 checked in full-text tables and four at abstract level). These counts do not mean 87 fully validated studies. Additional estimates were extracted from papers to preserve relevant null or uncertain results and wage comparisons that the Graph sample missed.

## Job security
The evidence distinguishes perceived insecurity, fixed-term contracts, daily labour, multidimensional precariousness and gaining work after unemployment. They are not interchangeable exposures. In the German S-MGA cohort, the male job-insecurity estimate was OR 2.47 (1.37–4.48); the female estimate was 1.48 (0.88–2.50). The sex difference itself has not been tested here. Low-wage estimates in the same models were 3.79 (1.64–8.72) and 1.19 (0.63–2.22), respectively. These overlapping, differently defined contrasts do not establish that wages matter more than insecurity. See Tables 5–6: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8949946/

The Korean welfare-panel paper separates fixed-term work from daily labour. Its adjusted mixed-model depressive-symptom ORs are 1.12 (1.00–1.26) and 1.68 (1.44–1.95), respectively. Several publications share this panel, so their results cannot be treated as independent replications without checking participants and waves. See Table 3: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10134250/

Uncertainty is retained: the Scania cohort's precarious-employment estimate is 1.7 (1.3–2.3) for ages 35–44 and 0.9 (0.6–1.4) for ages 45–54. These are different age strata. The Finnish public-sector paper reports no association with onset or recurrence of depression-related disability, while episode duration is longer. Duration, symptoms, inpatient diagnoses and general mental health must remain separate outcomes. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5769038/ and https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24664298

## Working hours and shifts
Long hours, overtime, night work, rotating schedules, and clinical shift-work disorder are distinct exposures. In UK Biobank, shift versus nonshift work has an incident-depression HR of 1.22 (1.12–1.33), while night versus nonnight shifts among shift workers has an HR of 1.05 (0.90–1.21). Changing the comparator changes the question. See Table 2: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10425829/

Null or uncertain estimates were retained: Canadian nurses' rotation-specific depression intervals include 1, and male production workers' incident mental-health sickness absence has HR 1.03 (0.84–1.26). These are abstract-level checks pending full-text verification. They neither establish no effect nor negate studies using other outcomes and schedules. Sources: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29340621 and https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25945780

## Why no pooled estimate yet
No currently extracted group has cleared all gates: compatible exposure/comparator, same outcome and threshold, compatible design/metric, verified uncertainty, and independent sampling. The accompanying pooling-assessment table states the blockers for each proposed group. Do not pool simply because a number is labeled OR. One musculoskeletal paper uses a zero-inflated negative binomial model but labels outputs as OR; one Chilean paper models symptom scores rather than binary disease. Abstract/table discrepancies are held visibly for adjudication.

## Geography and country context
Study countries are assigned from population descriptions in titles, abstracts or checked methods. Unknown settings remain unresolved; author affiliations are not substituted. Coverage refers only to the screened priority sample. World Bank income groups use FY2027, retrieved 14 November 2026, and are not historical income classes at the time of each study.

ILOSTAT comparisons match indicator, year 2023, total sex and main-job hours, but retain national age coverage and survey differences. The US note specifies minimum age 16. The series supplies 15 of 22 requested settings; seven are absent for the selected year/sex. WDI supplies four country values; requests for additional countries returned errors and are labeled retrieval failures. Only Brazil, the UK and the US currently have both measures. The common 2023 context year does not align to every historical study period. Missing countries remain missing. WDI vulnerable employment describes own-account and contributing family workers and is not equivalent to perceived insecurity. Neither contextual measure is an effect estimate or proof of country-level health outcomes.

## GLD status
The public GLD manual and access rules were checked. Harmonized microdata inherit source-survey access restrictions; the public repository provides documentation and code, not unrestricted access to every survey. No authorized survey file has been provided or retrieved, so GLD microdata are not integrated. Required next inputs are an accessible country-survey-year file, its sampling weights, harmonization version, units and employment definitions. Source: https://worldbank.github.io/gld/Support/A%20-%20Guides%20and%20Documentation/GLD%20Manual%20Files/GLD%20content,%20storage,%20and%20access.html

## Policy interpretation
The reviewed studies justify investigating insecure contracts and demanding schedules alongside pay. They do not identify a universal best intervention or quantify the effect of changing policy. Prospective designs strengthen temporal ordering, but confounding, self-selection, attrition and exposure misclassification remain. Formal risk-of-bias assessment and independent extraction checks are still required before strong recommendations.
