Does a full moon make for a busy night?

The made-up moon is a placebo. We wrote the prediction down before we looked, and a referee checked the result. That procedure is what we built.

Open here. Read the three captions aloud. On a busy night someone says it must be a full moon. We checked New York's records for the days around every full moon, and nothing changes. Then we made up a moon that is full every 23 days, and it gives the same numbers as the real one. Then say: the made-up moon is a placebo, the same test run where no effect can exist. Every test in this project is registered before the data are read, has a placebo where one is possible, and is checked by a referee who has not seen our reasoning. Figures if asked: around a full moon, shootings 1.007 (0.966 to 1.049), ambulance incidents 0.997 (0.991 to 1.004), injury crashes 1.002 (0.987 to 1.018); the 23-day moon gives 1.008, 0.998 and 1.002. The System graph holds 60 findings on the moon from 36 studies, and they disagree.
