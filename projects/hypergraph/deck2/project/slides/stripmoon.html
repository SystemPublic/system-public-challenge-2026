An example · a belief everyone has heard

Does a full moon fill the emergency room? We asked the graph, then the city.

One morning: 60 findings from the graph, a prediction registered before the test, three nulls, and a referee who found something nobody predicted.

Open with this. Everyone knows the belief. The System graph holds 60 findings on lunar variables from 36 studies, and they point in different directions: 1.042 for crash transports in Japan, 0.85 for homicides in Finland, 1.007 for cardiac arrest. We registered a prediction of no effect with equivalence bounds, then ran it: shootings 1.007 (0.966 to 1.049), ambulance incidents 0.997 (0.991 to 1.004), injury crashes 1.002 (0.987 to 1.018). A made-up 23-day cycle gives the same numbers. The referee then moved the window to every day of the cycle and found shootings lower four days before the full moon (0.914, z = -3.9). Nobody predicted it, so by our rules it decides nothing. That last frame is the point of the whole project: a result found by searching is a conjecture, and the procedure is what keeps it from becoming a belief.
