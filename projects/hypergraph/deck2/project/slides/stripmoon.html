A strip for a common belief

The full moon: the graph holds studies on both sides, and New York's record is a narrow null.

A thick arrow with a struck circle is a precise estimate of no effect. A made-up 23-day cycle gives the same numbers.

The graph has 60 findings on lunar variables from 36 studies, among them 1.042 for crash transports in Japan (Onozuka 2018), 0.85 for homicides in Finland and 1.007 for cardiac arrest. The claim moon-full-emergencies was registered before the test was run, as a prediction of no effect with equivalence bounds. For the three days around each full moon: shootings 1.007 (0.966 to 1.049) over 2006 to 2025, ambulance incidents 0.997 (0.991 to 1.004) over 2017 to 2026, and injury crashes 1.002 (0.987 to 1.018) over 2014 to 2025. The Japanese 1.042 is for full-moon nights; over three whole days it would be about 1.004, which the crash interval does not exclude. A referee reproduced the numbers.
