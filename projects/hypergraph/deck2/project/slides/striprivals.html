A strip with rival conjectures

Five reasons heat might raise shootings, and a test that narrows them

Registered this morning before the counts were read. Outside 1.17 per 5 °C, inside 1.01, on 24,429 shootings over 20 years. A referee widened our interval within the hour.

The literature offers at least five reasons. Two act through behaviour: heat brings people outdoors where they meet, and heat raises drinking. Three act on the body: short temper (a laboratory meta-analysis finds no reliable effect), sleep loss, and medicines that impair cooling. Some of them disagree about shootings indoors. Two claims were registered on 14 November before the dated counts were fetched. From 2006 to 2026 outside shootings rise 1.174 (1.155 to 1.192) per 5 °C on 20,958 incidents and inside shootings 1.009 (0.975 to 1.044) on 3,471; the ratio is 0.861 (0.828 to 0.896, the referee's corrected interval). A bodily effect that follows people indoors is not seen. The test does not separate more contact outdoors from a direct effect of heat felt outdoors, because most indoor spaces are cooled. A short search found no peer-reviewed estimate of this split for shootings; a 2018 newspaper analysis of Philadelphia showed the same pattern, and Australian assault data show indoor assault rising with heat.
