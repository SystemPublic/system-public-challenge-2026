Inspection scores and food poisoning: a placebo, then a new study on heat

Irwin 1989 found a fivefold outbreak risk after poor scores in Seattle; Jones 2004 found no association in 167,574 Tennessee inspections. Our registered test: 311 food-poisoning complaints are 1.12 times as frequent (0.96 to 1.32) in the year after a poor score, and the placebo, noise complaints, 1.67 (1.11 to 2.52). The claim is unsettled. Aik 2026 (Singapore, PMID 41483715, not in the graph) finds salmonellosis 1.10 (1.01 to 1.20) after heatwaves and campylobacteriosis 0.87. No study from 2020 to 2026 linking temperature to inspection violations was found, so that arrow is a conjecture.
