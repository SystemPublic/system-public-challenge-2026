Use 1 · The graph changes the shape of a hypothesis

Shootings rise with heat. The usual explanation, more people outdoors, stops working on hot days.

1.15

Shootings per 5 °C in New York, and 1.13 in Chicago

27 °C

Noise complaints and bicycle counts stop rising here. Findings in the graph from exercise physiology predicted this flattening.

1.05

The rise from 27 to 33 °C once night shootings are counted with the night they fall in (0.99 to 1.12)

A third of shootings happen between midnight and 06:00. Counting them with the previous day altered three estimates and no verdict. In four hot cities assaults stop rising at about 35 °C and do not fall (not a blind test: the series were read before registration).

The graph had one finding on heat and shootings and none on mechanism, but it had findings that heat reduces walking. That changed the prediction from "outdoor activity rises" to "outdoor activity flattens while shootings keep rising". The city data showed the flattening. The referee round then found the midnight issue, which shrinks the hot-end excess.
