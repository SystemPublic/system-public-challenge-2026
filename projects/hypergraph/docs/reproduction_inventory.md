# Inventory of city reports for a reproduction track

This file lists New York City and New York State government reports published between 2025 and 2026 whose main numbers come from data the public can download. It was compiled on 12 November 2026 for the reproduction track, in which a report's number is registered with its stated method and a tolerance and then recomputed from the city's own data, in the way a replication study recomputes a paper's result.

No NYC data rows were queried while this inventory was compiled. The Socrata catalog API and each dataset's metadata endpoint (`/api/views/<id>.json`) were read to identify dataset ids, column names and update times. Reports were read from the agencies' web pages and PDFs. Numbers below are quoted as the reports state them, with the page or section. A number marked "secondary" was read in press coverage and not in the agency's own document.

## Method of selection

A report qualified if it was issued by a city or state body, was published from 2022 onward, and stated at least one specific number drawn from data that are on NYC Open Data, data.ny.gov (MTA), or the Environment and Health Data Portal. Each candidate was given a data-access verdict. "Open" means every input to the headline number is published at row level. "Partly open" means some inputs are published but at least one (a model baseline, a camera list, a monitor network) is not. "Restricted" means the inputs are vital records, an internal database, or data available only on request. "Unknown" means the report does not say.

The ranking used four tests: whether the data are fully open, whether the method is stated (definitions, date range, geography, exclusions), whether there is a crisp number to recompute, and whether the topic connects to research and to the project's existing claims (heat, congestion pricing, crashes and speed cameras, construction, 311, asthma).

## Academic studies that disagree with a city report

The DOHMH evaluation of August 2026 found no significant change in pollution in the congestion zone. Fraser, Park, Lu, Tayarani, Deng and Gao (npj Clean Air, 8 December 2025) used 42 monitors over 518 days and reported that average daily maximum PM2.5 in the zone fell by 3.05 µg/m³, 22% below a projected 13.8 µg/m³, with declines of 1.07 µg/m³ across the five boroughs and 0.70 µg/m³ across the metropolitan area (Cornell summary). The two studies differ in period (six months against a full year), outcome (daily maximum against annual or seasonal means), monitors and counterfactual. A Columbia University and South Bronx Unite analysis (May 2026, not peer reviewed) reported increases at four of 19 monitors near South Bronx expressways; press reports give the average change inconsistently. The project's own claim mob-crz-air found NO2 in the zone at +0.9% (−2.2 to +4.2), which agrees with DOHMH and not with Fraser et al.

The MMR's taxi speed indicator fell from 6.9 to 6.5 mph between fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026, while the MTA reported weekday speeds in the zone up 4% against 2024 and the project's claim mob-crz-traffic found taxi and FHV speeds in the zone up 5.7% relative to the rest of the city. These are not the same measure: the MMR series is yellow taxis only, daytime weekdays, without a comparison area, and fiscal 2025 already contains six months of tolling. Reproducing the MMR number is a direct way to locate the difference.

For speed cameras, DOT reports a 95% fall in daily notices per camera since 2014 and 14% fewer injuries at cameras installed in 2022. A longitudinal study from NYU Tandon (2025) reported a 75% drop in speeding tickets by the end of 2021 and a 14% fall in crashes. The two speeding figures cover different periods and use different measures.

## Ranking for a reproduction that can be registered and run in a few hours

The first choice is the DCP Housing Production Snapshot. The data are open at project level, the number (38,682 units completed in new buildings in calendar 2025) is crisp, and the topic is the project's construction question. The snapshot contains an internal inconsistency (63,805 against 66,805 units in the pipeline) that a reproduction can resolve, and its statement that 80 to 90 percent of permitted jobs complete within four years is the subject of the claim construction-four-year-completion. The portal now holds a later release than the one the snapshot used, so a tolerance must allow for revision; DCP's 2024 count moved from 33,974 to 33,859 between releases, a revision of 0.3%.

The second choice is the State Comptroller's NYC311 report. Appendix A states the period, the grouping column and the exclusions, and Appendix B gives exact counts by complaint type and year. The report's text says HPD received "over 246,700" heat and hot water complaints in 2024, while Appendix B gives 264,746; a reproduction will show which figure the data support. The report warns that the city adds back-dated records, so a small upward drift is expected.

The third choice is the MMR indicator for taxi speed in the Manhattan CBD. The definition is precise and the trip records are open, but trip records give only zone endpoints and times, while DOT may compute speed from GPS traces. A registration should state the approximation (trips with pickup and drop-off both in CBD taxi zones, speed as total distance over total time, or the mean of trip speeds) and accept a wider tolerance. The result bears directly on the conflict between the MMR series and the MTA and project results on congestion pricing.

The fourth choice is DOT's count of 2025 traffic deaths (205, with a borough split that sums to 205) and preliminary injuries (47,557). The NYPD person table is open, but DOT's fatality file is reconciled and later deaths are added, so the open data will probably not match exactly. The size and direction of the gap is itself a result about which public record of traffic deaths to trust, and it matters for the crash claims in the bridges.

The fifth choice is the per-vehicle distribution in DOT's speed camera addendum: 3,742 vehicles with 30 or more notices in 2024 and 2,794 in 2025. The camera violation table is open at row level with plate and violation code. The vehicle key and the treatment of dismissed notices must be registered as stated assumptions. The research literature on recidivist speeders and the project's claim mob-speedcam-247 give the context.

The Comptroller's heat report is a close sixth; it is open but its definitions are in the full PDF, which should be read before registration. The MTA one-year figures, the shed count, the DOHMH air quality evaluation and the heat mortality report are not suitable for a few-hour reproduction, because their baselines are modelled, their dates are ambiguous, or their data are available only on request or from vital records.

## Ambiguities to settle in each registration

For the DCP snapshot, a registration should name the Housing Database release (25Q4 from BYTES, or the current portal copy), the completion rule (`compltyear` = 2025 for job type New Building), the unit field (`classaprop` for new buildings, or `units_co`), whether jobs with `job_status` other than completed are included, and whether hotel and other non-Class A units are excluded.

For the 311 report, a registration should name the date field (`created_date`), the time zone and year boundary, whether the agency is joined to the complaint type exactly as in Appendix B, and how the drift since May 2025 is allowed for. The total of 3,428,573 depends on the exclusion of dormant complaint types; the per-type counts do not.

For the CBD speed, a registration should state the CBD boundary in taxi zones (the MTA list `yfdc-w5jh` or zones wholly south of 60th Street), the trip filter (both ends in the CBD, or pickups only), the holiday list, the treatment of zero or implausible distances and durations, and the aggregation.

For traffic deaths, a registration should state whether deaths are counted from the crash table (`number_of_persons_killed`) or the person table (`person_injury` = Killed), the crash date range, and that 2025 means crash date in calendar 2025.

For the camera counts, a registration should state the violation code text for school-zone speed cameras, the vehicle key, the year field (`issue_date`), and whether violations with any status are counted.
