Concordance strip

Each row is one claim. The horizontal axis runs from −1, where the city data contradict the direction the research reports, through 0, where they cannot tell, to +1, where they agree. The coloured bar is the outcome's interval on this scale and the mark is its central value. A bar that crosses the centre line is a claim the city data have not settled, whatever its verdict word. The shaded band shows the side on which the registrant bet before seeing the data, and the column at the right gives the registrant's prior probability that the claim would be confirmed. Rows without a bar are registered but not yet decided, or could not be tested. Select a row to see its detail below.

One claim

Research estimate beside the city estimate

The upper row is the research side: the outcome's summary of the graph evidence, or, where there is no summary, the graph findings it cites. The lower row is the estimate from New York City's data. Each line is a 95% interval and the dashed line is no effect. When both rows use the same unit they share one axis. When the units differ, each row has its own axis, and only the direction and whether each interval crosses no effect can be compared.

Bridge path

A bridge is a link that no single dataset holds. It is built from a chain of link claims, each tested on its own. The path runs from top to bottom through the concepts each link connects. Each link is coloured by its verdict and carries a small strip with its interval on the agreement scale, so the weakest link is the one whose interval reaches furthest to the left. A dashed connector marks two neighbouring links that use different concepts at the joint, where the bridge assumes they are the same thing.

Provenance

The time line shows the order of events: the registration, with the hash that fixes its text, then the fetch of each dataset, then the outcome. A dataset fetched before the registration is marked, because the bet may then have seen the data. Below it are every dataset with its version and exact query, and every graph finding the claim rests on, with links to PubMed.

Research needed

This map collects every "research needed" entry and every "what would settle it" statement across the claims, and sorts them by the kind of data or study design they call for. Rows are claims and columns are kinds of research. A filled cell is a research-needed entry, darker for higher priority, and the number counts the entries. An outlined cell is a settle statement only. The kinds are assigned from the words of each entry, so one entry can fall in more than one column. The list under the map gives the entries in full.
