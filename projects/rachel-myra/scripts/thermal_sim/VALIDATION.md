# Validation of the Heat-Safe Delivery thermal model

Generated by `python -m thermal_sim.write_validation_md` from `validation_results.json`. Re-run `python -m thermal_sim.validation` to reproduce (deterministic seeds, ~4 min).

## Short version

* **What we checked:** the share of transit time a mailed package spends outside 20–25 °C (USP controlled room temperature) and outside 15–30 °C, simulated with 300 Monte-Carlo summer shipments per shipping speed driven by real hourly LaGuardia weather (June–August 2024 and 2025). We compared those shares with the two published logger studies cited in `docs/supply-chain.md`.
* **Fit after calibration:** 3-day ground 30.7% vs published 43.1%; next-day 26.0% vs 13.6%. Root-mean-square error: 12.4 percentage points.
* **Where it fails:** the model **does not reproduce the ~3x gap between ground and next-day** that JAPhA reports, at any value of the two free parameters. We think the real next-day network spends more of its time in conditioned air hubs and aircraft holds than our itinerary assumes.
* **ASHP 2020 check (all 48 packages left 20–25 °C at some point):** reproduced. Nearly every simulated package leaves 20–25 °C at some point (table below).
* **Not validated at all:** doorstep physics (porch sun and shade, mailbox). No measured porch study exists; `docs/supply-chain.md` lists this as a gap. Doorstep numbers rest on handbook heat-transfer values, not on fitted data.

## Published targets

| source | what | number |
|---|---|---|
| JAPhA 2023 (Chowdhury et al.; 36 packages, NJ/CA/TN, 3 carriers) | summer, % of transit time out of range, 3-day ground | 43.1% |
| same | summer, next-day | 13.6% |
| ASHP Midyear 2020 (Univ. of Utah; 48 packages, 6 US sites; conference) | packages that ever left 68–77 °F (20–25 °C) | 48 of 48 |
| same | summer, % of time out of range (range across packages) | 27–54% |

The notes do not say which band JAPhA's "out of range" refers to. Two facts point to 20–25 °C. First, the same paper reports 68.3% out of range on average across seasons, which is only possible if winter is near 100%. Second, ASHP uses 68–77 °F. We therefore calibrate to the **20–25 °C reading** and report the 15–30 °C reading as an alternative.

## Monte-Carlo design (all assumptions)

| draw | distribution |
|---|---|
| ship day | uniform over 3 June–25 August, 2024 and 2025 |
| delivery time (local) | next-day uniform 9–17 h; 3-day uniform 10–19 h |
| doorstep | porch_shade 45%, porch_sun 25%, lobby 20%, mailbox_sun 10% |
| time before retrieval | lognormal, median 1 h, sigma 0.8, capped at 8 h |
| packaging | poly 50% / padded 50%; contents start at 22 °C |
| itinerary | `itinerary.py` templates: next-day = truck → hub overnight → delivery truck from 06:30; 3-day ground = alternating hub and line-haul trailer legs, including one daytime trailer leg per day |
| transit window | from carrier pickup (16:30) until the package is brought inside |

## Calibration (free parameters only)

Free parameters (marked `free` in `PARAMETERS.md`):
* `truck_beta`: how much the sun warms truck and trailer cargo air above outdoor air (°C per kW/m² of horizontal sun).
* `hub_f`: how much of the outdoor temperature swing reaches the sorting hubs (0 = fully conditioned at 22 °C, 1 = unconditioned).

Grid: truck_beta ∈ [0.0, 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0], hub_f ∈ [0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0]. Objective: RMSE against the two JAPhA numbers.

## Honest reading

1. **The adopted values sit at a corner of the grid** (truck_beta=0: truck cargo at outdoor air temperature; hub_f=0: hubs held at 22 °C). They are the least-heat corner. The fit asks for as little heating as the model allows, and the real optimum may lie outside the physically allowed range. Most likely cause: the published packages travelled NJ/CA/TN routes (ASHP: 6 US sites), and much of that is milder than a NYC summer. The calibration absorbs that climate difference. **The adopted model therefore probably runs cool for NYC.** Read its pre-delivery temperatures as a lower bound.
2. **The 15–30 °C reading lands on physically plausible values** (truck_beta=15, hub_f=1: sun-heated cargo, unconditioned hubs) with a similar error (RMSE 13.8 vs 12.4). The demo file includes the hero case under these values as `hero.sensitivity_hot_truck`, the upper case.
3. **Ground vs next-day:** published 43.1% vs 13.6%. With every parameter combination we get roughly equal shares for both speeds. The model has no separate air network, and our next-day itinerary includes a full morning on a delivery truck. Even a morning-only delivery window does not close the gap (sensitivity table). We do not claim the model reproduces the next-day benefit. The "upgrade to next-day" recommendation in the pre-ship check comes from simulated physics (less time in trucks and hubs), not from this published ratio.
4. **ASHP's every-package-leaves-range finding is reproduced** under all parameter sets, because NYC summer air alone exceeds 25 °C for much of the day.
5. **No calibration touched the doorstep.** Porch, mailbox and apartment parameters are handbook values or labeled assumptions. The pre-ship check and the doorstep advice should be presented as physics-based estimates, and the logger hardware demo (docs/supply-chain.md §3) is how they would be measured.
