# Rare and Extreme Ways Heat Alters Drug Pharmacokinetics and Causes Metabolic/Renal Toxicity

Scope note: covers absorption, accumulation, and metabolic/renal harms inside the body. It does not cover drug storage or degradation, or thermoregulatory toxidromes such as NMS or anticholinergic heat stroke. Budget: about 15 search calls, so many identifiers come from search-result metadata (URLs/DOIs/PMIDs shown in the result) and were not opened in full. Anything recalled from memory and not seen in a result this session is marked UNVERIFIED in the Gaps sections.

## Transdermal patches and heat (fentanyl, buprenorphine, nicotine, nitroglycerin, clonidine, rivastigmine, estradiol, scopolamine)

### Takeaway
Heat increasing patch absorption is best documented for fentanyl, with a human PK crossover study (Cmax and AUC about 3x higher in the first 4 h under heat), a fatal sauna case with forensic findings, and a coma-plus-full-thickness-burn case from sunbathing. Buprenorphine has a human heating-pad study (+26 to 55% plasma levels) plus an in vitro–in vivo correlation. Nicotine has sauna and local-heat studies. Rivastigmine has label-level warnings only. For nitroglycerin, clonidine, estradiol and scopolamine I found nothing primary in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Fentanyl, human PK (crossover; pharmacokinetic study).** Ashburn et al., J Pain 2003, PMID 14622685. A 25 µg/h patch with local controlled heat for the first 4 h gave Cmax and AUC almost 3 times higher than without heat during that window. Over 36 h there was no significant difference in Cmax, AUC or Tmax, so the effect is front-loaded and transient. — [Ashburn 2003 record](https://opact.sdu.edu.tr/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c438t-627b2486825cd15580161df25670b3dff9427a875920981508cbe25a4fa945162/Description)
- **Fentanyl, labeling/PK model.** A PK model cited in the US labeling predicts that a rise in body temperature to 40 °C (104 °F) could raise fentanyl serum concentrations by about 33%. This is the basis for fever warnings. — [Medical Letter](https://secure.medicalletter.org/TML-article-1318c)
- **Fentanyl, in vitro permeation (mechanistic).** Zhang et al. 2020, PMID 32702372 (PMC7644256). The flux increase depended on when heat started and how long it lasted. Heat applied earlier, or sustained heat, gave higher flux. — [PMC7644256](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7644256)
- **Fentanyl, in vitro vs in vivo bioequivalence (FDA-sponsored-type protocol, NCT02486016).** 1 h of 42 ± 2 °C heat at 11 h or 18 h after application. The search summary says the in vivo heat enhancement was larger than the in vitro one. No PMID located. — [ClinicalTrials.gov protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/16/NCT02486016/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)
- **Fentanyl, fatal sauna case (case report; forensic).** Am J Forensic Med Pathol 2020, DOI 10.1097/PAF.0000000000000565. Accidental fatal overdose attributed to increased patch uptake in sauna heat. Postmortem femoral blood fentanyl was 15 µg/L. — [AJFMP abstract](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/amjforensicmedicine/abstract/10.1097/paf.0000000000000565~death-in-sauna-associated-with-a-transdermal-fentanyl-patch?redirectionsource=fulltextview)
- **Fentanyl, sun exposure (case report; extreme).** J Med Case Rep 2012, 6:220, DOI 10.1186/1752-1947-6-220 (PMC3427044). A 77-year-old woman sunbathing with fentanyl patches developed life-threatening coma and full-thickness sunburn. The authors call it the first report of coma plus full-thickness burns with the fentanyl transdermal system. The article also cites a separate patient who developed overdose symptoms after placing a heating pad over the patch. — [JMCR](https://jmedicalcasereports.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1752-1947-6-220); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3427044/)
- **Fentanyl, fever (case report, secondary citation only).** A blog cites a 2020 case of fever-induced encephalopathy in an elderly woman on a fentanyl patch that reversed with naloxone. I could not identify the primary paper. A lower-quality source claims no fever-only cases exist, which conflicts with this. — [CoffeeClinicals blog (secondary)](https://coffeeclinicals.com/fever-fentanyl-patch-comes-with-a-catch/)
- **Regulator warning, Finland (Fimea).** Transdermal opioid patches should not be exposed to sauna, heat pads or warm baths, which can cause life-threatening poisoning. This matters in a high-sauna-use population. Fimea also advises checking heat instructions for non-opioid patches. — [Fimea](https://fimea.fi/en/-/fimea-issues-a-warning-wearing-an-opioid-patch-in-the-sauna-may-cause-life-threatening-poisoning)
- **Buprenorphine, human heat study (PK study; label).** 20 healthy volunteers wore a 10 µg/h Butrans patch with a heating pad. Plasma buprenorphine rose 26–55% during 7 h of intermittent heat and stayed raised for up to 5 h after the heat was removed. A separate study at 38 °C for 3 h with another system showed no increase in Cmax or AUC, which is a contradictory or null result. The label warns that fever may increase skin permeability and that heat may cause overdose and death. — [FDA Butrans label 2013](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/021306s016lbl.pdf); [RxReasoner Butrans pharmacology](https://www.rxreasoner.com/monographs/butrans/pharmacology)
- **Buprenorphine, IVIVC (in vitro + modelling).** Pharm Res 2023, PMID 37095369, DOI 10.1007/s11095-023-03487-z. Human skin from 4 donors at 32 vs 42 °C. The heat-induced rise in flux and cumulative permeation was reasonably consistent with the in vivo rise, and the authors built a Level A IVIVC. — [PubMed 37095369](https://www.pubmed.gov/37095369/)
- **Nicotine (PK/physiology studies).** In an earlier study, subjects in a sauna wearing a nicotine patch had significantly higher skin blood flow and transdermal uptake. A later controlled trial used 43 °C local heat with residue analysis. Heat had to be intermittent because continuous heat could melt the patch adhesive, a device-failure confounder. The same paper's introduction cites raised nitroglycerin absorption from patches at high ambient temperature, but I did not retrieve the primary nitroglycerin study. — [PMC3175749](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3175749/)
- **Rivastigmine (label only).** The Canadian monographs say not to expose the patch to saunas, hot tubs, heating pads, heat lamps or excessive sunlight, and note that fever can also raise absorption. The US Exelon label allows bathing and hot weather but says to avoid long exposure to external heat sources. A 2020 trial protocol (NCT03915626) states that almost all available transdermal systems carry heat warnings. — [Health Canada monograph](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00075541.PDF); [Drugs.com Exelon patch PI](https://www.drugs.com/pro/rivastigmine-patch.html); [NCT03915626 protocol](https://ClinicalTrials.gov/ProvidedDocs/26/NCT03915626/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across the fentanyl and buprenorphine data, heat effects are largest early in the wear period and persist hours after the heat is removed (depot in the skin). So the overdose window can begin after a sauna or hot bath ends, for example when the person sleeps.
- The extreme fentanyl outcomes (sauna death, coma with burns) involve elderly users and prolonged whole-body heat. Opioid-naive or frail users plus sustained environmental heat appear to be the tail-risk combination.
- For nitroglycerin, clonidine, estradiol and scopolamine, heat warnings likely rest on the general transdermal mechanism and older studies rather than on documented harms.

### Gaps
- No primary study or case found this session for **clonidine, estradiol or scopolamine** patch heat exposure. Nitroglycerin is supported only by a secondary citation in PMC3175749.
- The **FDA fentanyl patch public health advisory/safety communications** (2005, 2007, and 2012 heat warnings) are recalled from memory, UNVERIFIED this session.
- No documented **hot tub** fentanyl death in the peer-reviewed literature was found. Only a law-firm document lists hot tubs, so it is not citable.
- Primary citation for the 2020 fever-induced fentanyl encephalopathy case is not identified.
- PMIDs for the AJFMP sauna death and the JMCR 2012 case were not confirmed (DOIs are confirmed from results).

## Subcutaneous/intramuscular absorption with heat (insulin, GLP-1, heparin)

### Takeaway
The classic sauna–insulin study is small: 8 patients, 85 °C sauna, insulin absorption up 110% and glucose falling. Later local-heating work suggests hexamer dissociation, not perfusion, limits absorption of concentrated insulin, so heat effects vary by formulation. A case report describes DKA after a sauna in a pump user, the opposite failure mode. I found no heat-absorption data for GLP-1 agonists or heparin.

### Cited Findings
- **Insulin, sauna (PK/clinical study, n=8).** Koivisto, BMJ 1980 (PMC1601709). Two 25-min sessions at 85 °C sped insulin absorption by 110% compared with room temperature, and blood glucose fell in step. The author advised a snack or a lower dose before sauna. — [PMC1601709](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1601709)
- **Insulin, local heating (PK study in healthy men, 2011).** For high-concentration short-acting insulin, tissue perfusion was not rate-limiting. Hexamer dissociation was suggested as the main limit, implying a formulation-dependent heat effect. — [PMC3230259](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3230259)
- **Insulin pump plus sauna leading to DKA (case report, Diabetes Care 1999).** Heat-related insulin aggregation in the infusion device was proposed. The authors advised against sauna while wearing an infusion device. This sits on the device boundary and is noted for the other researcher. — reported in search summary (same result set as [PMC1713913](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1713913))

### Inferences
- Heat can push insulin both ways: faster depot absorption gives hypoglycemia, while pump/device insulin degradation gives hyperglycemia or DKA. The tail-end risk is in pump users.

### Gaps
- The exact citation (PMID) for the 1999 Diabetes Care sauna/pump DKA case was not confirmed. UNVERIFIED.
- No studies found on heat and **GLP-1 agonists, heparin/LMWH, or IM injectables** absorption.
- Hot tub insulin studies and exercise-in-heat insulin absorption studies (e.g., Berger/Koivisto exercise-limb studies) are recalled from memory but not retrieved. UNVERIFIED.

## Drug accumulation from heat-induced reduced renal/hepatic perfusion (lithium, digoxin, metformin, DOACs, others)

### Takeaway
Lithium has the most heat-specific evidence: a poison-center correlation with ambient temperature, a large Dutch serum-level seasonality dataset, and a case of heat illness leading to AKI and lithium intoxication that needed dialysis. For metformin, digoxin and dabigatran I found only AKI/dehydration case reports. One fatal dabigatran case explicitly blames summer low fluid intake. I found no heat-wave-specific cohort for aminoglycosides, vancomycin, gabapentinoids, methotrexate, colchicine, phenytoin or valproate.

### Cited Findings
- **Lithium, ambient temperature (retrospective poison-center study, Arizona, 2022).** Chronic lithium toxicity frequency correlated with rising ambient temperature. Season alone did not correlate. — [University of Arizona repository](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/669553)
- **Lithium, serum levels vs temperature (observational, Netherlands).** 41,102 lithium levels from 3,054 patients. Mean levels differed significantly by season and by temperature category. Full results were not read. — [Wilting et al., Utrecht thesis chapter](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/27401/wilting_07_theimpactofenvironmentaltemperature.pdf?sequence=2)
- **Lithium, heat illness (case report, Japan 2018).** Dehydration and AKI from heat-related illness unmasked lithium intoxication. Treatment needed about 6 h of hemodialysis, tremor lasted about 2 weeks, and the patient was discharged on day 25. — reported via [APAMT 17th Congress poster](https://apamt.org/assets/Uploads/Congress-Abstracts/17th-Congress/17th_Poster_Presentation_21.pdf) (congress abstract, not peer-reviewed article)
- **Renal function, heat and antihypertensives (hospital cohort, >65 y).** In elderly inpatients on thiazides and/or ACEi/ARB, ambient temperature was significantly associated with rising creatinine. This is the substrate for accumulation of renally cleared drugs. — [PLoS One 2016, PMID 27992525](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27992525/)
- **Dabigatran (case report, fatal).** A 77-year-old on dabigatran died of fatal bleeding diarrhoea. The authors suggest that seemingly benign conditions, such as diarrhoea and low fluid intake in summer, can impair renal function and cause accumulation. — [PubMed 25534224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25534224/)
- **Dabigatran, acute renal failure leading to life-threatening bleeding (case report).** — [PubMed 25293571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25293571/); also [Wychowski & Kouides 2012, DOI 10.1345/aph.1q747](https://doi.org/10.1345/aph.1q747)
- **Metformin-associated lactic acidosis with dehydration AKI (case reports).** One case had creatinine rising from 1.7 to 8 mg/dL and lactate of 14.9 mmol/L. Another was a 64-year-old with dehydration-driven AKI. A third notes higher risk with ACEi plus loop diuretics. None are heat-specific. — [JECCM case](https://jeccm.amegroups.org/article/view/4889/html); [PMC6424540](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6424540/); [PMC11271817](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11271817/)
- **Digoxin in acute renal failure (case report).** Acute-on-chronic toxicity. Delayed clearance of digoxin-Fab complexes in renal failure can cause rebound toxicity, which needed plasma exchange after Fab failed. Not heat-specific. — [Cureus](https://www.cureus.com/articles/427528-digoxin-toxicity-in-renal-failure-resolution-with-plasma-exchange-after-fab-therapy-failure.pdf)
- **Lithium plus AKI plus NMS (case report).** AKI-induced lithium toxicity with concomitant NMS. Viral gastroenteritis was the trigger, not heat. — [PMC11460575](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11460575/)

### Inferences
- The renal-accumulation pathway (heat leads to volume loss, lower GFR, and drug accumulation) is mechanistically shared by lithium, metformin, dabigatran, digoxin, aminoglycosides and vancomycin. Only lithium has direct temperature-exposure data. For the others the heat link is extrapolated from dehydration and AKI case reports.
- Digoxin rebound after Fab in renal failure is an especially extreme tail: heat-AKI could plausibly produce Fab-refractory toxicity. This is an inference, with no heat case found.

### Gaps
- No heat-wave cohorts found for **aminoglycosides, vancomycin, gabapentin/pregabalin, methotrexate, colchicine, phenytoin, valproate, apixaban/rivaroxaban**.
- Japanese lithium heat case: no PMID located (congress abstract only).
- Netherlands lithium temperature paper (Wilting et al.): the published journal PMID was not confirmed.

## Rhabdomyolysis (statins, antipsychotics, colchicine with heat/exertion)

### Takeaway
Direct drug-plus-heat rhabdomyolysis evidence is thin. There is one Korean case of statin plus exercise plus sauna. Antipsychotic rhabdomyolysis is well signalled in FAERS (quetiapine and olanzapine dominate) but not tied to heat in the literature found. Exertional heat stroke can cause bimodal (late second-surge) rhabdomyolysis.

### Cited Findings
- **Statin plus exercise plus sauna (case report, Korea 2008).** Rhabdomyolysis resolved after stopping the statin, exercise and sauna. The authors framed sauna heat as compounding statin myotoxicity. — [CiteSeerX PDF](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=3ca694607f865adfa1e8df803f6371a7e94b4722&repid=rep1&type=pdf)
- **Atypical antipsychotics, FAERS pharmacovigilance (Sci Rep 2025).** Quetiapine was the most reported drug in rhabdomyolysis cases (n=655, 27.75%), followed by olanzapine (n=621, 26.31%). Olanzapine had the strongest disproportionality signal. — [Sci Rep, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-95700-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-95700-9)
- **Exertional heat stroke, bimodal rhabdomyolysis (case report and review, J Intensive Care 2016).** A second CK surge came during recovery, after steroid dose reduction. — [DOI 10.1186/s40560-016-0193-9](https://jintensivecare.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40560-016-0193-9)

### Inferences
- Combined statin and heat or exertion risk is biologically plausible (additive myocyte stress) but is supported only by single case reports.

### Gaps
- No colchicine plus heat rhabdomyolysis case was found.
- No cohort linking statin or antipsychotic use to rhabdomyolysis on hot days was found.

## Electrolyte extremes (hyponatremia, hypokalemia/QT, hypernatremia)

### Takeaway
Heat-related hyponatremia is well quantified in Sweden. Risk rises above about 15 °C, is almost tenfold on the hottest days, and is about 15-fold in people aged 80 and over during heat waves. Thiazides and SSRIs account for a large share of hyponatremia admissions, and a case-crossover pharmacovigilance study shows drug-induced hyponatremia reports rise with temperature.

### Cited Findings
- **Heat-related hyponatremia (Swedish nationwide register study, JCEM 2022).** DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgac103. Hyponatremia admission risk rises rapidly above 15 °C (a threshold effect), is almost tenfold on the hottest days, and is about 15x in people aged 80 and over during heat waves. — [JCEM](https://dx.doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac103); [PDF](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-pdf/107/6/e2388/43739065/dgac103.pdf)
- **Drug-induced hyponatremia and temperature (case-crossover pharmacovigilance, IJERPH 2017).** DOI 10.3390/ijerph14070827. Reported drug-induced hyponatremia rose with higher temperature in the warm season. — [IJERPH](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph14070827)
- **Thiazides and SSRIs (Karolinska thesis).** About 30% of hyponatremia hospitalizations may be caused by thiazides and SSRIs alone. Elderly patients, those with low BMI, and those also taking thiazides are at higher risk on SSRIs. — [KI thesis PDF](https://openarchive.ki.se/articles/thesis/From_drugs_to_climate_associations_between_external_factors_and_profound_hyponatremia/29064197/1/files/58427545.pdf)
- **Singapore warm-season hyponatremia in older patients.** Partly linked to thiazide use. The thiazide warm-versus-cold difference was not significant. — [Proc Singapore Healthc, DOI 10.1177/2010105819839373](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2010105819839373)

### Inferences
- The extreme tail is elderly, low-BMI patients on a thiazide plus SSRI during heat waves, likely from water loading combined with impaired free-water excretion.

### Gaps
- No heat-specific data retrieved for **desmopressin, MDMA (other than general knowledge), carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine** hyponatremia.
- No studies found on heat-related **hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia leading to torsades** with QT-prolonging drugs, or on drug-associated **hypernatremia** in heat.

## SGLT2 inhibitors (euglycemic DKA, volume depletion) and sulfonylurea hypoglycemia in heat

### Takeaway
Ramadan data show modest volume-depletion signals and no reported SGLT2i DKA during Ramadan, per a review. No study combining ambient heat, SGLT2i and DKA was found. Diuretics plus SGLT2i is an independent admission risk factor.

### Cited Findings
- **Ramadan reviews and real-world data.** In a 2024 systematic review, hypovolemia/dehydration rose significantly in only one study. A narrative review states that SGLT2i DKA has not been reported during Ramadan. In a Bangladesh empagliflozin study, volume depletion was 2.6% vs 1.8% (not significant) with no admissions. IDF-DAR guidance advises taking the dose at Iftar, extra fluids, and caution in elderly or renal-impaired patients. — [Thieme review PDF](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0044-1791484.pdf); [DMSO, DOI 10.2147/DMSO.S380544](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.2147/DMSO.S380544)
- **Saudi PASS (NCT03764631).** Measuring ketoacidosis, volume depletion and dehydration with empagliflozin vs DPP-4i, with a Ramadan 2019 analysis. No published results found. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03764631)
- **SGLT2i DKA with prolonged glucosuria and relapse (case series, n=11).** — [PMC7170458](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7170458/)
- **Diuretics as an independent risk factor for SGLT2i-related admissions, including ketoacidosis, in diabetic HF patients.** — [PMC11588271](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11588271/)
- **General heat-hospitalization drug signals (cohort/self-controlled).** In the Medicare heatwave study, ACEi/ARB, anticholinergics, antipsychotics and loop diuretics were linked to heat hospitalization. Loop diuretic RR was 1.54 in dementia, 1.64 in heart failure and 1.84 in MI. A sequence symmetry analysis found the highest dehydration/heat admission risk after starting ACEi plus diuretic. One diabetes GP case-crossover study was null for diuretics and antipsychotics. — [PLoS One 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665); [Kalisch Ellett 2016, DOI 10.1111/jcpt.12418](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpt.12418); [Pharmacy scoping review, DOI 10.3390/pharmacy14030074](https://doi.org/10.3390/pharmacy14030074)

### Gaps
- No heat or heat-wave study of **SGLT2i euglycemic DKA**; this would be mechanistic inference only.
- No heat-specific **sulfonylurea hypoglycemia** data were retrieved.

## Anticoagulation/thrombosis in heat

### Takeaway
Warfarin INR seasonality is inconsistent: one study shows a summer low and autumn high, others show nothing clinically meaningful. Dabigatran harm from summer dehydration is case-level only. No heat-hemoconcentration thrombosis drug data were retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Warfarin seasonality.** In 800 outpatients, mean INR was lowest in summer and highest in autumn. A St. Louis study of 499 patients found no deterioration in summer. A more recent retrospective study found no clinically significant seasonal variation. In neonates, only outdoor temperature significantly influenced INR. — [Karger Pharmacology](https://karger.com/pht/article-abstract/32/4/151/387574/Intensity-of-Long-Term-Treatment-with-Warfarin-Is-Influenced-by-Seasonal-Variations); [Am J Med](https://www.amjmed.com/article/S0002-9343(01)00889-0/fulltext); [PubMed 33903173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33903173/); [PMC4950811](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4950811/)
- **Dabigatran, summer low fluid intake (see accumulation section).** — [PubMed 25534224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25534224/)

### Gaps
- No studies found on heat hemoconcentration causing thrombotic events in drug users (e.g., estrogen-containing contraceptives, erythropoietin).

## Drug–heat effects on seizure threshold / blood–brain barrier

### Takeaway
Dravet syndrome (SCN1A) combines heat sensitivity (hot baths and exertion are triggers) with sodium-channel-blocker aggravation. Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin and lamotrigine worsen seizures. In mice, clobazam, valproate and stiripentol raise the temperature threshold for seizures. No human data on drug-altered heat thresholds were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Triggers include hot baths, exertion and fever, and sodium-channel-blocking ASMs aggravate seizures (clinical review/GeneReviews).** — [GeneReviews (SCN1A/GEFS+)](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/gene/gefs); [Dravet Foundation brochure](https://dravetfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Could-it-be-Dravet-Syndrome-Brochure-5.29.22.pdf)
- **Mouse models.** In Scn1a+/− mice, sodium channel blockers were ineffective or worsened hyperthermia seizures. In Scn1a A1783V mice, clobazam and the clobazam+valproate+stiripentol combination raised seizure temperature thresholds. — [Ann Clin Transl Neurol, DOI 10.1002/acn3.413](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1002/acn3.413); [PMC8360068](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/581d20bd2bf710d6835f65101a5386aa/PMC8360068.pdf)

### Gaps
- No human study found on heat-induced blood–brain barrier changes altering CNS drug levels.
- No pharmacoepidemiology found on heat waves and breakthrough seizures with altered ASM levels (phenytoin/valproate free fraction).
