# Drug-heat harms in special populations, niche exposures, skin/sun, and wearables

Method note: I ran about 17 web searches. Items marked [VERIFIED-SEARCH] were confirmed in search results with the link shown. Items marked [UNVERIFIED – from background knowledge] were not confirmed in this session. The report writer should not cite them as fact without checking. No PMID or DOI is given unless a search result showed it.

## Pregnancy, neonates/infants, breastfeeding

### Takeaway
Almost no studies test drug-by-heat interaction in pregnancy. The one positive finding is that low-dose aspirin appeared to weaken the link between heat and preterm birth (secondary analysis of a trial, 2026). A large Israeli cohort found medication interactions were not significant. I found no studies on tocolytics, antihypertensives or antiemetics plus heat, or on infants or breastfeeding plus heat.

### Cited Findings
- Global Network ASPIRIN trial, secondary analysis (JAMA Netw Open, May 2026). Without aspirin, each 1°C rise in average daily humid heat during pregnancy was linked to about 5% higher odds of preterm birth. This rise was not seen in people who took low-dose aspirin early in pregnancy. However, heat was linked to perinatal mortality among aspirin takers, especially in malaria-endemic areas. Evidence type: secondary analysis of an RCT. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [BIDMC press release](https://bidmc.org/news-stories/all-news-stories/news/2026/05/bidmc-study-aspirin-preterm-births-heat-risk); [AKU record](https://scholars.aku.edu/en/publications/aspirin-and-preterm-birth-among-pregnant-people-with-increased-he/). Lay coverage saying aspirin "eliminated" the risk overstates the result — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/motherhood/news/lowdose-aspirin-may-counter-heatlinked-premature-birth-risk-study-finds-ee070ece-eeb7-4043-a2ea-5142abd97a21/)
- Southern Israel cohort (131,599 births, 2005–2019). It tested pre-pregnancy comorbidities, prior preterm birth and prescribed medications as modifiers of the temperature–preterm birth link. Medication interactions were not statistically significant. Prior preterm birth marked a high-risk group. Evidence type: retrospective cohort. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [HUJI record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/ambient-temperature-and-preterm-birth-interaction-by-maternal-med/)
- National Birth Defects Prevention Study. Certain medications that affect thermoregulation amplified the effect of extreme heat events on congenital heart defects in the US Southwest. Evidence type: case-control. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Interaction-of-maternal-medication-use-with/9984293312602771)

### Inferences
- The aspirin signal is plausibly anti-inflammatory or anti-placental-dysfunction. It is not evidence that aspirin is safe in heat. The perinatal mortality signal points the other way.

### Gaps
- No study found on magnesium sulfate, nifedipine, terbutaline or indomethacin (tocolytics), labetalol or methyldopa, or ondansetron, metoclopramide or promethazine combined with heat.
- No study found on infants on medications (for example anticholinergic antispasmodics, antiepileptics, diuretics in bronchopulmonary dysplasia or cardiac disease) in heat. None found on drug transfer in breast milk changing during heat or maternal dehydration.

## Children and adolescents

### Takeaway
The best pediatric evidence is for topiramate (and, by analogy, zonisamide) causing reduced sweating and overheating. Children are over-represented in these reports, and the FDA warned about it in 2003. For ADHD stimulants, the evidence is case reports plus sports-medicine reviews. No population-level risk estimate exists.

### Cited Findings
- Topiramate, Israeli pilot (Ben-Zeev et al., 2003). Nine patients had reduced sweat on pilocarpine iontophoresis testing, and 8 of them were under 16. Only 3 had symptoms of heat intolerance. Evidence type: small pilot. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [TAU record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/oligohydrosis-and-hyperthermia-pilot-study-of-a-novel-topiramate-/)
- Topiramate, US series. Seven children aged 5–14 overheated 2–19 months after starting topiramate. Symptoms resolved when the dose was cut or the drug stopped. Evidence type: abstract case series. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [AES abstract](https://aesnet.org/abstractslisting/hypohidrosis-and-hyperthermia-in-a-series-of-children-associated-with-topiramate-therapy)
- Topiramate, Turkish retrospective series (İncecik et al., 2012). 22 of 173 patients had symptoms of reduced sweating, and 68.2% of those were under 6 years old. Evidence type: retrospective. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Turk J Pediatr](https://turkjpediatr.org/article/download/1678/1658)
- Health Canada and FDA (2003). Reports involved mainly children, and the warning advised close monitoring for reduced sweating and raised body temperature. Evidence type: regulatory. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Health Canada](https://www.healthycanadians.gc.ca/recall-alert-rappel-avis/hc-sc/2003/14236a-eng.php)
- Extreme cases: an adult with status epilepticus and severe hyperthermia, and a fatal heat stroke in an adult on topiramate (case report). [VERIFIED-SEARCH, details not read] — [AJEM](https://ajemjournal.com/article/S0735-67571300455-5/fulltext)
- ADHD stimulants: a young athlete on amphetamine/dextroamphetamine had heat stroke, which the authors partly attributed to the drug (Cureus 2024, DOI 10.7759/cureus.68020). Evidence type: case report. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC11430407](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11430407/)
- A review lists amphetamines, methylphenidate, ephedra and alpha agonists among drugs linked to exertional heat illness. It says methylphenidate has been shown to raise body temperature during activity in warm environments. Evidence type: review. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC4596225](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4596225)
- Jordan McNair (Univ. of Maryland football, died of heat stroke in 2018) had been prescribed Vyvanse. No causal link has been established. Current stimulant labels do not mention heat stroke or exertion. Evidence type: journalism. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/espn/story/_/id/41440938/athletes-adhd-anxiety-meds-increase-heat-stroke-risk)
- NFHS (high school sports body) guidance on ADHD athletes and heat. Evidence type: grey literature or advocacy. — [NFHS](https://nfhs.org/stories/heat-related-illness-and-adhd-athletes)
- Zonisamide causing reduced sweating in children (Knudsen 2003, Pediatr Neurol). [UNVERIFIED – from background knowledge]

### Inferences
- The mechanism proposed for topiramate and zonisamide is carbonic anhydrase inhibition in sweat glands. This is a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No pediatric cohort or case-control study of stimulants, antipsychotics (for example risperidone, aripiprazole in youth) or sedating antihistamines and heat illness.
- No studies found on school-based medication administration and heat.

## Older adults and dementia

### Takeaway
The main US study (Layton 2020, Medicare) found heat-sensitizing drugs raised heat-related hospitalization risk modestly all summer (rate ratios 1.16–1.37). It found no meaningful synergy with heatwaves. That "no interaction" result goes against common assumptions.

### Cited Findings
- Medicare beneficiaries aged 65+ with chronic conditions (including dementia), June–August 2007–2012. Drug classes studied: diuretics, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, beta-blockers, stimulants, antihypertensives. Several classes had rate ratios of 1.16 (95% CI 1.00–1.35) to 1.37 (1.14–1.66) for heat-related hospitalization even without a heatwave. Heatwaves raised rates by 21–33% in every drug group, but the authors found no meaningful synergistic interaction. PLoS ONE 2020;15:e0243665. Evidence type: cohort. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC7728169](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7728169/)

### Gaps
- No study found on cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil and similar: they cause sweating and slow heart rate) as a heat risk modifier.
- No study found on nursing-home heat events broken down by medication. The 2003 European heatwave literature on nursing homes was not searched this session.

## Chronic disease groups

### Takeaway
Dialysis patients face clearly higher heat risk of hospitalization and death, but drug-specific data are missing. MS has the most interesting drug-heat data: small studies suggest 4-aminopyridine (dalfampridine) may widen heat tolerance. Transplant (tacrolimus) and heat: no study found. For HIV, sickle cell disease, CF, spinal cord injury, Parkinson's/DBS and heart failure, I found no drug-by-heat studies this session.

### Cited Findings
- ESRD/dialysis, case-crossover study (n=7,445; Boston, Philadelphia, New York). Extreme heat events were linked to same-day hospital admission (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.13–1.43) and death (RR 1.31, 1.01–1.70). Risk was higher with heart failure, COPD or diabetes. JAMA Netw Open 2019, PMID 31397862. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC6692691](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6692691)
- Remigio et al., Environ Res 2021 (n=1,981 hemodialysis patients). Higher temperature was linked to lower blood pressure, changes in weight gain between sessions, and more hospitalization and death. These are possible pathways that interact with antihypertensives and fluid management. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC8962569](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8962569)
- MS and 4-AP: an 11-patient relapsing-remitting MS crossover in a 48°C water-perfusion suit. On 4-AP, resting core temperature and core temperature at sweat onset were lower than on placebo. The authors suggest 4-AP may widen the range of core temperature rise before symptoms worsen. Evidence type: small RCT. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [IJESAB / WKU](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijesab/vol2/iss8/2)
- Older trials: Davis et al. 1990 (20 temperature-sensitive men) and Bever et al. 1994 (8-patient crossover) found 4-AP improved deficits in temperature-sensitive MS. There is a dose-dependent seizure risk above 10 mg twice daily. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Ovid, Bever 1994](https://www.ovid.com/journals/neur/pdf/00006114-199406000-00013~the-effects-of-4-aminopyridine-in-multiple-sclerosis); [Scilit, Davis](https://scilit.net/publications/2b4053038a130662705e54c4aaae2427)
- A Utah thesis tested 4-AP against heat-induced worsening of eye-movement mismatch (internuclear ophthalmoparesis) in MS. Results were not visible in the search. [VERIFIED-SEARCH, results unread] — [Utah](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/ark:/87278/s6dj8nd1)
- Transplant: no study found linking heat to tacrolimus or cyclosporine levels or acute kidney injury. Indirect evidence: a 2022 cohort of 34 transplant patients with COVID-19 found raised tacrolimus levels in 59%, with acute kidney injury in 65% of those with levels above 8 vs 29% of those with lower levels. This shows illness-driven level rises can cause harm. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Transpl Int 2022](https://www.frontierspartnerships.org/journals/transplant-international/articles/10.3389/ti.2022.10127/full)

### Inferences
- Dialysis heat risk probably works partly through blood-pressure-lowering drugs and fluid balance (Remigio's pathway findings), but no drug-stratified analysis was found.

### Gaps (true gaps for this session)
- HIV/antiretrovirals and heat: not found.
- Sickle cell disease (hydroxyurea, voxelotor) and heat: not found. Heat is a recognized trigger of crises, but there is no drug-interaction study.
- Cystic fibrosis (CFTR modulators and sweat chloride, salt loss in heat): not searched or found. Note: CFTR modulators lower sweat chloride, so heat-salt-loss risk could in theory change [UNVERIFIED inference].
- Spinal cord injury (anticholinergics for bladder, poikilothermia), Parkinson's (levodopa; withdrawal-hyperpyrexia syndrome; DBS battery failure), heart failure (diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors and heat): not searched in depth. No studies found.

## Outdoor and occupational, military, athletes, Hajj/Ramadan

### Takeaway
Drug-specific evidence in these groups is mostly case reports and reviews. I found no case-control study of prescribed medications and exertional heat illness in the military. Hajj literature names multiple medications as risk factors but does not quantify specific drugs.

### Cited Findings
- Military: a soldier (parachutist) had exertional heat stroke with convulsions and urine positive for amphetamine, probably from a contaminated supplement. The authors call it the first such case in the military setting. Evidence type: case report. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [J Arch Mil Med 2023](https://brieflands.com/articles/jamm-129649.html)
- A French military case-control study of exertional heat stroke focuses on psycho-cognitive factors, not drugs. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.08.12.22278401v3.full.pdf)
- Hajj: reviews name old age, diabetes (31% of hospitalized pilgrims in one Makkah hospital), multiple medications that interfere with thermoregulation, crowding and fever. No specific drugs are quantified. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Rev Environ Health 2021, PMID 34714988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34714988/); [DOI 10.1515/reveh-2021-0097](https://doi.org/10.1515/reveh-2021-0097)

### Gaps
- Agricultural and construction workers: drug-specific heat studies not found this session.
- Gulf War deployment: no drug-heat data found. (Pyridostigmine bromide and heat was not searched.)
- Firefighters and medications: no study found (see the wearables section for physiology-only work).
- Ramadan fasting plus diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors or lithium in heat: not searched or found.

## Unhoused, incarcerated, and substance use disorder populations

### Takeaway
Prison heat epidemiology exists. It shows higher death and suicide-watch rates, and authors cite psychotropic drug use as a reason for vulnerability, but these studies do not stratify by medication. For methadone and buprenorphine, I found no heat-mortality study, only commentaries and studies on drug-related ED visits and heat.

### Cited Findings
- Texas prisons (Skarha et al., JAMA Netw Open 2022). Over two decades, the study estimated more than 250 heat-attributable deaths. Death risk was 15% higher on extreme heat days, and 0.7% higher per °F above 85°F. Prisons with air conditioning showed no heat-mortality link. Medication was not analyzed. [VERIFIED-SEARCH, via secondary reporting] — [Grist](https://grist.org/equity/texas-prisons-heat-air-conditioning-study/); [Texas Observer](https://www.texasobserver.org/heat-kills-hundreds-in-texas-prisons/)
- US state and private prisons (Skarha et al., PLoS ONE). Heat was linked to higher total and heart-disease mortality. The authors note that people on psychotropic medications are at higher risk. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PDF](https://www.suicideinfo.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Heat-related-mortality-in-U.S.-state-and-private-prisons.pdf)
- Louisiana prisons (Cloud et al., JAMA Netw Open 2023). Extreme heat was linked to more suicide-watch incidents (IRR 1.30). [VERIFIED-SEARCH, via secondary] — [Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/publications/extreme-heat-and-suicide-watch-incidents-amont-incarcerated-men-aug-2023)
- California, 3.4 million drug-related ED visits (2005–2019). Higher temperature was linked to all outcomes, including opioid overdose (OR about 1.079 per temperature increment). [VERIFIED-SEARCH, via search snippet only] — [CDC Stacks](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/208114/cdc_208114_DS1.pdf)
- A British Columbia case-crossover study looked at temperature and opioid, cocaine and amphetamine deaths. Results were not retrieved. — [CMAJ Open submission](https://www.cmajopen.ca/highwire/filestream/89989/field_highwire_adjunct_files/0/open-2021-0291-original-submission.pdf)
- New York State clinician guidance on extreme heat and people who use drugs. Evidence type: grey literature. — [NY DOH](https://health.ny.gov/diseases/aids/consumers/prevention/oduh/docs/extreme_heat_clinicians.pdf)

### Gaps
- No study found on methadone or buprenorphine patients in heatwaves (for example QT prolongation, or clinic access during heat).
- Heat litigation over psychiatric medications in prisons (for example Texas cases such as Cole v. Collier): not verified this session [UNVERIFIED].
- Unhoused people plus medications in heat: no study found.

## Skin and sun: phototoxicity, skin cancer, sweat disorders, patches, hand-foot syndrome

### Takeaway
The strongest skin evidence is drug-induced cancer risk with sun exposure: voriconazole (lung and stem-cell transplant patients) and hydrochlorothiazide (Danish registries, leading to regulatory action). Heat-driven fentanyl overdose from patches is on the label, with deaths. I found no study of seasonal or heat effects on hand-foot syndrome.

### Cited Findings
- Voriconazole and cutaneous SCC in lung transplant patients:
  - Singer 2012 (PMID 22484291): 28% absolute increase in SCC risk at 5 years. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC3371090](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3371090)
  - Kolaitis 2017 (PMID 27678492): HR 1.75 for cumulative exposure. [VERIFIED-SEARCH]
  - Hamandi 2017 (PMID 28898527): 900-patient multicenter study. [VERIFIED-SEARCH]
  - US registry of 9,599 lung recipients (PMID 32401271): risk rises with cumulative exposure. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC7221851](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7221851)
  - Meta-analysis, JAAD 2019 (PMID 30130598): RR 1.72 (1.09–2.72) for longer use. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [curehunter](https://www.curehunter.com/public/pubmed30130598.do)
- Hydrochlorothiazide and non-melanoma skin cancer, especially dose-dependent SCC (Pedersen et al., JAAD 2018, PMID 29217346). Lip cancer comes from Pottegård et al., J Intern Med 2017. The UK MHRA issued a Drug Safety Update in November 2018. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29217346/); [MHRA](https://gov.uk/drug-safety-update/hydrochlorothiazide-risk-of-non-melanoma-skin-cancer-particularly-in-long-term-use)
- Fentanyl patch: the boxed warning says heat on the application site (heating pads, electric blankets, saunas, hot tubs, sunbathing) increases absorption and "has resulted in fatal overdose". Fever and exercise-related heating are also listed. The FDA has kept receiving death reports (2007 update). [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [FDA archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090117201018/http:/www.fda.gov/cder/drug/infopage/fentanyl/default.htm); [Prescriber label](https://prescriberpoint.com/therapies/fentanyl-bf02ffd)
- Capecitabine hand-foot syndrome: incidence 22–77% depending on the study, with risk factors such as female sex. No data on season or heat. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC9300399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9300399/)
- Drug-induced reduced sweating (topiramate, zonisamide): see the children section.
- Amiodarone (blue-gray skin, phototoxicity), fluoroquinolones, doxycycline, NSAIDs (piroxicam), retinoids, methotrexate (UV recall), St John's wort (hypericin): well known in reviews, but no specific citations were verified this session [UNVERIFIED].
- Burns from metal-containing patches occur under MRI, not sun. Do not overstate a sun or heat burn link without a source [UNVERIFIED].

### Gaps
- Miliaria or heat rash caused by drugs: no studies found.
- Hand-foot syndrome and ambient heat: no study found, so this is a true gap.

## Wearables (heart rate, skin temperature, estimated core temperature, PSI) in people on medications

### Takeaway
Heart-rate-based core temperature estimators (ECTemp and similar) have been validated only in healthy soldiers, athletes and firefighters. They underestimate at high temperatures (false negatives above 39°C). I found no validation in people taking beta-blockers, stimulants, anticholinergics or other drugs that change heart rate. This is a clear evidence gap.

### Cited Findings
- MIT Lincoln Lab independent check of the heart-rate core temperature algorithm and of PSI (physiological strain index) computed from it. It is described as a field tool, not a clinical one. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [DTIC AD1034034](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1034034.pdf)
- Elite athletes in heat (Front Sports Act Living 2022, DOI 10.3389/fspor.2022.882254). ECTemp had many false negatives above 39.0°C. The authors say it "should not solely be used" to spot athletes at risk. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.882254/pdf)
- Firefighters (Sci Rep 2023). Turnout gear did not reduce the estimate's validity. An earlier live-fire study found it unsuitable compared with pill thermometers. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [PMC10728086](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10728086)
- In PPE at rest or low work rates, the estimate underestimated rectal temperature. [VERIFIED-SEARCH] — [JOEH](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15459624.2016.1161199)

### Inferences
- Beta-blockers blunt heart rate, and stimulants and anticholinergics raise it. Heart-rate-based core temperature models would therefore be expected to underestimate or overestimate in these users. This is untested.

### Gaps
- No validation of wearable heat-strain estimators in medicated, elderly, MS, dialysis or pregnant populations was found.
