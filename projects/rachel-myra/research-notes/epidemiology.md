# Population-level epidemiology: medications and heat-related morbidity/mortality

Verification notes: PubMed and PMC pages were blocked by captcha during this session. Bibliographic data were checked through the Europe PMC REST API where noted ("EPMC-verified"). Anything taken only from secondary summaries, or from memory, is flagged. About 18 tool calls were used, so coverage of Korea/Taiwan/China, UK CPRD and Kaiser data is incomplete (see Gaps).

## Q1. Major studies and effect sizes (France 2003, Australia, Ontario, US Medicare, UK, Japan)

### Takeaway
The best-known quantitative signals come from three places: the 2003 French heat wave case-control studies (antipsychotics, anticholinergics, antidepressants), an Australian veterans sequence-symmetry analysis (ACEi plus diuretic combination is highest at 2.79), and a US Medicare self-controlled case series (antipsychotics, anticholinergics, loop diuretics). Effect sizes are mostly moderate (RR/OR 1.2 to 2.8). Large unadjusted ORs (4 to 6) come only from small samples.

### Cited Findings
**France, 2003 heat wave**
- Martin-Latry K et al., 2007, *Eur Psychiatry* 22(6):335-338, PMID 17513091. Matched case-control, Bordeaux. Cases were 56 ED admissions for hyperthermia or heat stroke (4% of 1,405 ED admissions; mean age 83). Controls were local residents with a reimbursed prescription in July 2003. Results: anticholinergic drugs OR 6.0 (95% CI 1.8-19.6), antipsychotics OR 4.6 (1.9-11.2), anxiolytics OR 2.4 (1.3-4.4). — [PubMed 17513091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17513091/); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/psychotropic-drugs-use-and-risk-of-heatrelated-hospitalisation/54FC76B216AB19AEFFAE4F30A21E1D99)
- Nordon C et al., 2009, *Am J Geriatr Psychiatry* 17:1059-1067. Population-based case-control using the French national social-security database. Population: aged 70-100; 9,531 deaths during the heat wave and 2,093 before it, each matched to survivors on age, sex and chronic illness. Any psychotropic use was linked to about 30% higher risk of death during the heat wave, with a dose-response (aOR for linear trend 1.25, 1.21-1.29). Antidepressants aOR 1.71 (1.57-1.86); antipsychotics aOR 2.09 (1.89-2.35); **anxiolytics/hypnotics aOR 0.85 (0.79-0.91), i.e. protective**. The association varied with external temperature. — [secondary summary via search, climahealth/NIEHS](https://tools.niehs.nih.gov/cchhl/index.cfm/detail/1508); [Santé publique France context](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/climat/fortes-chaleurs-canicule/reportsummary/study-risk-factors-death-among-older-adults-living-home-during-august-2003-heat-wave-july-2004). FLAG: the ORs came from secondary abstracts. I could not retrieve a PMID or DOI for Nordon 2009 through Europe PMC, so check against the journal.
- Background: the 2003 heat wave caused about 14,802 excess deaths in France between 1 and 20 August. The InVS/Santé publique France home-dwelling and nursing-home case-control surveys looked at drug consumption as a risk factor. — [Santé publique France](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/climat/fortes-chaleurs-canicule/reportsummary/study-risk-factors-death-among-older-adults-living-home-during-august-2003-heat-wave-july-2004)
- One search snippet attributes to a French 2003 matched ED case-control a finding that antiepileptics, anti-Parkinson drugs, psycholeptics, diuretics and antidiabetic drugs were associated with higher odds of death. — [scoping review search snippet, Tews et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/pharmacy14030074). FLAG: the primary study was not identified, so treat this as unverified.

**Australia**
- Kalisch Ellett LM et al., 2016, *J Clin Pharm Ther* 41:503-507, PMID 27378245, DOI 10.1111/jcpt.12418 (EPMC-verified). Prescription sequence symmetry analysis of 6,700 Australian veterans admitted for dehydration or heat-related illness, 2001-2013 (DVA claims). Initiation risk ratios ranged from 1.17 (SSRIs) to 2.79 (ACEi plus diuretic combination product). Significantly raised risk followed initiation of anticoagulants, cardiovascular medicines, NSAIDs, antipsychotics, antidepressants and anticholinergics. **Null: anticonvulsants, anti-Parkinson agents, hypnotics, anxiolytics, antihistamines.** CIs are not in the abstract. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27378245&resultType=core&format=json); [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2016-07-meds-hospitalization-dehydration-heat-linked-illness.amp); [MedShadow](https://medshadow.org/drug-updates-recalls/drug-safety/drugs-with-highest-risk-for-dehydration-or-heat-related-illness/)
- Mangoni AA et al., 2017, *Adv Ther*, PMID 29116624, DOI 10.1007/s12325-017-0629-1 (EPMC-verified). Older medical inpatients in South Australia. "Triple whammy" use (diuretic plus NSAID plus ACEi/ARB) did **not** predict in-hospital AKI within 48 h: outside heat waves OR 0.79 (0.34-1.83), during heat waves OR 1.02 (0.21-2.97). It also did not predict length of stay or mortality. This is a **null conflict** with the dehydration and AKI concern. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29116624&resultType=core&format=json); [Flinders](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/the-concomitant-use-of-diuretics-non-steroidal-anti-inflammatory-/)
- Westaway K et al., 2015, *J Clin Pharm Ther*, PMID 26073686, DOI 10.1111/jcpt.12294. This is a commentary or review listing high-risk classes (diuretics with ACEi/ARB, anticholinergics, psychotropics), with no effect sizes. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26073686&resultType=core&format=json)

**Ontario, Canada (AKI)**
- McTavish RK, Richard L, McArthur E et al., 2018, *Am J Kidney Dis*, PMID 29074166, DOI 10.1053/j.ajkd.2017.07.011 (EPMC-verified). Matched case-control, ICES data, adults 66 years and older (mean age about 80). 52,913 AKI cases and 174,222 controls. Heat periods aOR 1.11 (1.00-1.23), about 182 excess AKI cases per 100,000 person-years in the warm season. **No medication-specific estimates are in the abstract.** — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29074166&resultType=core&format=json); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/9186318). NOTE: the task's "Layton et al. Ontario diuretics/ACEi/ARB AKI" seems to conflate two papers. Layton 2020 is a US Medicare study, and the Ontario AKI paper is McTavish 2018. I did not verify any Ontario drug-by-heat AKI interaction paper.

**United States, Medicare**
- Layton JB, Li W, Yuan J, Gilman JP, Horton DB, Setoguchi S, 2020, *PLoS One*, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0243665. Self-controlled case series of 9,721 fee-for-service Medicare beneficiaries aged 65 and older with chronic conditions (HF, MI, COPD/asthma, stroke, insulin-treated diabetes, dementia, CKD) and a heat-related hospitalization, June-August 2007-2012.
  - Medication effect outside heat waves: anticholinergics RR 1.16 (1.00-1.35); antipsychotics RR 1.37 (1.14-1.66).
  - Subgroups: loop diuretics in dementia 1.54 (1.06-2.24), in HF 1.64 (1.20-2.23), in MI 1.84 (1.13-3.00); antipsychotics in CKD 1.68 (1.18-2.40), in diabetes 1.83 (1.19-2.82).
  - **Beta-blockers and stimulants were not associated.**
  - Heat waves raised heat-related hospitalization by 21% (ACEi/ARB users, RR 1.21, 1.07-1.38) to 33% (anticholinergic users, 1.33, 1.14-1.55).
  - **There was no meaningful heat wave × medication synergy.** — [PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665); [RTI](https://www.rti.org/publication/heatwaves-medications-and-heat-related-hospitalization-older-medicare-beneficiaries)

**United Kingdom**
- Page LA, Hajat S, Kovats RS, Howard LM, 2012, *Br J Psychiatry* 200:485-490, PMID 22661680, DOI 10.1192/bjp.bp.111.100404 (EPMC-verified). Time-series of deaths in England, 1998-2007, using primary care records (GPRD/CPRD) of patients with mental illness. Mortality rose 4.9% (2.0-7.8) per 1°C above the 93rd percentile of annual temperature. The greatest risk was in younger patients and those with primary substance misuse. The KCL press release says risk was "more marked" in people taking antipsychotics. Medication classes were antipsychotics, antidepressants, hypnotics/anxiolytics and other. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:22661680&resultType=core&format=json); [KCL press release](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/archive/news/ioppn/records/2012/june/service-users-at-risk-during-hot-weather). The medication-specific % estimates were not retrieved because the full text was inaccessible.
- A 2017 UK case-crossover of heat-related GP consultations in people with diabetes found **no association** with diuretics, antipsychotics, antidepressants or anticholinergics. — [Tews et al. 2026 scoping review, Pharmacy 14(3):74](https://doi.org/10.3390/pharmacy14030074). FLAG: primary citation not retrieved. It is likely a Hajat et al. CPRD paper but unverified.

**Japan**
- Wong AYS et al., 2024, *Psychol Med*, PMID 39648665, DOI 10.1017/S0033291724002824 (EPMC-verified). Self-controlled case series, Ibaraki prefecture claims, 2014-2021.
  - Severe mental illness: heat wave and heat-related illness IRR 1.48 (1.40-1.56) in antipsychotic-prescribed periods vs 1.45 (1.35-1.56) unprescribed; interaction p=0.53.
  - Depression: IRR 1.54 (1.46-1.64) on antidepressants vs 1.64 (1.57-1.71) off; p=0.33.
  - Weak evidence for MI and delirium.
  - **The psychotropic itself added no detectable risk; the risk lay in the condition.** — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1017/S0033291724002824&resultType=core&format=json); [PMC11769904](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11769904/)
- Japan national case-crossover of heatstroke ambulance dispatches, 2015-2019: the temperature effect was modified by air-conditioning prevalence, with no drug data. — [PMC12182763](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12182763/)

### Inferences
- The two large French and US datasets agree on antipsychotics (OR/RR about 1.4 to 2.1) and anticholinergics. The small Bordeaux ED study's ORs of 4 to 6 are probably inflated by small numbers and crude matching.
- The diuretic/RAAS signal is strongest for **combination** initiation (Kalisch Ellett) and for loop diuretics in cardiac and dementia patients (Layton). Mangoni (null on AKI) and McTavish (no drug strata) show that the AKI-specific drug evidence is thinner than guidance implies.

### Gaps
- Korea (NHIS), Taiwan (NHIRD), mainland China and Kaiser Permanente drug-heat studies were **not found** in this session's searches. Do not claim they exist without further search.
- No verified Ontario ICES study gives diuretic/ACEi/ARB × heat interaction ORs for AKI.
- CIs for Kalisch Ellett 2016 class estimates, and the Layton 2020 Table 3 values for ACEi/ARB, loop diuretics and beta-blockers outside heat waves, were not retrieved.

## Q2. Psychiatric drugs (antipsychotics, lithium, antidepressants, benzodiazepines/hypnotics) and schizophrenia

### Takeaway
Antipsychotics are the most consistent drug signal across France, the US and Australia. The best-designed recent study (Japan SCCS, 2024), however, found that heat-illness risk tracked the psychiatric condition, not the prescription. Benzodiazepines/hypnotics conflict: they were harmful for hospitalisation in Bordeaux and protective for death in France nationally. Population data on lithium were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Antipsychotics: Bordeaux heat hospitalisation OR 4.6 (1.9-11.2); French national mortality aOR 2.09 (1.89-2.35); Medicare RR 1.37 (1.14-1.66); Australian initiation significantly raised. — [PubMed 17513091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17513091/); [NIEHS summary](https://tools.niehs.nih.gov/cchhl/index.cfm/detail/1508); [PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665); [EPMC 27378245](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27378245&resultType=core&format=json)
- Antidepressants: French mortality aOR 1.71 (1.57-1.86); SSRIs had the smallest Australian initiation ratio (1.17). Japan SCCS showed no modification (IRR 1.54 on vs 1.64 off). — sources as above; [Wong 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1017/S0033291724002824&resultType=core&format=json)
- Anxiolytics/hypnotics:
  - Bordeaux hospitalisation OR 2.4 (1.3-4.4), harmful.
  - French national mortality aOR 0.85 (0.79-0.91), **protective**.
  - Australia: initiation of hypnotics and anxiolytics was null. — [PubMed 17513091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17513091/); [NIEHS summary](https://tools.niehs.nih.gov/cchhl/index.cfm/detail/1508); [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2016-07-meds-hospitalization-dehydration-heat-linked-illness.amp)
- Schizophrenia and mental illness: England had +4.9%/°C mortality above the 93rd centile (Page 2012), with greater excess in psychosis, dementia and substance misuse. — [Europe PMC 22661680](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:22661680&resultType=core&format=json)
- Bark NM (1982, *J Clin Psychiatry*, PMID 6126476) reviewed 51 heatstroke cases in psychiatric patients: 22 died, mean age 44, mostly male, mostly schizophrenia, most taking antipsychotics or anticholinergics. Case series only. Bark 1982 *Hosp Community Psychiatry* (PMID 6124500, DOI 10.1176/ps.33.6.474) is a discussion paper. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Bark%20N%22%20AND%20TITLE:%22heatstroke%22&resultType=core&format=json). FLAG: "Bark 1998" (likely *Psychiatr Serv* 1998, deaths of psychiatric patients during heat waves) was **not verified** in this session.
- Lithium: lithium appears in the Wong 2024 supplementary drug tables, but no lithium-specific estimate was retrieved. A CMAJ commentary cites dehydration-related lithium toxicity as a concern. — [CMAJ](https://www.cmaj.ca/custom-print/114703); [Cambridge supplement](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn%3Acambridge.org%3Aid%3Aarticle%3AS0033291724002824/resource/name/S0033291724002824sup001.docx)
- UK government guidance flags psychotropics as impairing thermoregulation, especially in schizophrenia and dementia. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mental-health-medications-and-extreme-weather)
- New: an abstract titled "Heatwaves and arrhythmia among antidepressant and antipsychotic users, France, 2016-2024" appeared in *Eur J Public Health* 2026 (Suppl 5), PMC13697566. Results were not available. — [Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Heatwaves%20and%20arrhythmia%22&resultType=core&format=json)

### Inferences
- The protective hypnotic/anxiolytic association in the French mortality study likely reflects selection or confounding. One possibility is that hypnotic users were healthier, community-dwelling patients. It should be presented as a conflict, not as evidence of benefit.
- The within-person SCCS designs (Japan, Medicare) show weaker or no drug × heat interaction than the between-person case-control designs (France). This is consistent with confounding by indication in the older studies.

### Gaps
- No population study with lithium-specific heat estimates was found.
- Page 2012 medication-stratified estimates were not retrieved.

## Q3. Less-known classes (antiepileptics, antiparkinsonian, opioids, antidiabetics, anticoagulants, statins, PPIs, laxatives, thyroid, anti-dementia, antiplatelets; polypharmacy/anticholinergic burden)

### Takeaway
Among the less-usual classes, only **anticoagulants** (Australia) and **NSAIDs** have a verified positive population signal. Antiepileptics and anti-Parkinson drugs conflict between studies. Stimulants and beta-blockers were null in Medicare. No population-level effect sizes were found for opioids, SGLT2 inhibitors, statins, PPIs, laxatives, thyroid drugs, anti-dementia drugs or antiplatelets.

### Cited Findings
- Anticoagulant initiation significantly raised dehydration/heat-illness admission risk in Australian veterans. This is an unexpected class, and the estimate is not in the abstract. — [EPMC 27378245](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27378245&resultType=core&format=json)
- Antiepileptics and anti-Parkinson agents: null on initiation in Australia. One unverified French 2003 snippet reports increased mortality odds for antiepileptics, anti-Parkinson drugs and antidiabetic drugs. — [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2016-07-meds-hospitalization-dehydration-heat-linked-illness.amp); [Tews 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/pharmacy14030074)
- Stimulants and beta-blockers were not associated with heat-related hospitalization in Medicare. — [PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665)
- SGLT2 inhibitors: the evidence is mechanistic (osmotic diuresis). A scoping review found them in only 1 label among 24 studies, and there are no outcome studies. — [Pharmacol Rev review](https://pharmrev.aspetjournals.org/article/S0031-6997(24)00797-X/fulltext); [Tews 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/pharmacy14030074)
- Opioids: guidance only (sedation, and misdiagnosis of overdose versus heat stroke), with no outcome data. — [Americares provider guide](https://www.americares.org/wp-content/uploads/HEAT_Medications_ForProviders_FINAL0904.pdf)
- Anticholinergic burden and polypharmacy:
  - A study of older adults hospitalised during heat waves found polypharmacy, potentially inappropriate medications and Anticholinergic Risk Scale were each associated with admission but **not with death**. It also found no difference in medication count or anticholinergic exposure in heat-wave versus non-heat-wave periods. (Likely Mangoni et al.; full citation not verified.) — [ResearchGate: Heat Waves, Drugs with Anticholinergic Effects, and Outcomes in Older Hospitalized Adults](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/302910863_Heat_Waves_Drugs_with_Anticholinergic_Effects_and_Outcomes_in_Older_Hospitalized_Adults)
  - Medicare anticholinergic users had the highest heat-wave RR (1.33, 1.14-1.55). — [PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665)
- Americares cites a study in which empiric potassium supplementation in furosemide users (≥40 mg/day) reduced risk during extreme heat. **Source study unverified.** — [Americares](https://www.americares.org/wp-content/uploads/HEAT_Medications_ForProviders_FINAL0904.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "less-known classes" are mostly supported by mechanism or labels, not by epidemiology. The report should say this plainly rather than imply that observational evidence exists.

### Gaps
- No verified population-level estimates were found for statins, PPIs, laxatives, levothyroxine, cholinesterase inhibitors/memantine, antiplatelets, opioids or SGLT2 inhibitors.

## Q4. Effect modification, null and protective findings, night/humid heat, minimum mortality temperature

### Takeaway
Within-person designs consistently find **no synergy** between heat waves and medication use: Medicare 2020, Japan 2024, and Mangoni 2017 for AKI. Medications seem to mark vulnerable people more than they multiply heat risk. Physiology meta-analysis also finds most psychotropics do not alter core temperature in heat.

### Cited Findings
- Medicare: "no meaningful synergistic interactions" between heat waves and medications. — [PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665)
- Japan: interaction p=0.53 for antipsychotics and p=0.33 for antidepressants. — [Wong 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1017/S0033291724002824&resultType=core&format=json)
- France: the psychotropic-death association was modified by external temperature level, which is the opposite of the SCCS findings. — [Santé publique France / scilit record](https://www.scilit.net/publications/811e8a83ae4d446eeb85a75deb498208); [NIEHS summary](https://tools.niehs.nih.gov/cchhl/index.cfm/detail/1508)
- A 2024 eClinicalMedicine systematic review and meta-analysis of trials found that antidepressants, anxiolytics, antipsychotics, diuretics and weak anticholinergics did not alter core-temperature responses during heat stress. The authors note that epidemiology cannot separate drug from disease. — [eClinicalMedicine 2024](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(24)00465-6/fulltext)
- A Medicare case-crossover (2025 preprint) gave OR 1.05 (1.03-1.06) for heat-related hospitalization from median to 95th-percentile temperature, holding PM2.5 fixed. It has no drug data. — [arXiv 2505.07662](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07662)

### Inferences
- The difference between "drug raises baseline risk" and "drug amplifies heat risk" is central. Most evidence supports the first. Evidence for the second comes mainly from the French 2003 case-control studies.

### Gaps
- No studies were found that test medication interactions with **night-time heat** or **humid heat** (wet-bulb/heat index), or that estimate shifts in **minimum mortality temperature** by medication use. This appears to be a genuine evidence gap, not only a search gap, but that is unconfirmed.

## Q5. Methodological limits

### Takeaway
Confounding by indication is the dominant problem. SCCS and sequence-symmetry designs control for fixed patient traits but not for time-varying illness severity. Exposure is based on dispensing, and outcomes on heat-coded diagnoses that undercount heat deaths.

### Cited Findings
- Epidemiological studies cannot untangle drug effects from the treated disease. — [eClinicalMedicine 2024](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(24)00465-6/fulltext)
- Kalisch Ellett's design compares medicine initiation with admission timing, so it captures initiation effects only. It cannot capture prevalent use and gives no CIs in the abstract. — [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2016-07-meds-hospitalization-dehydration-heat-linked-illness.amp)
- Martin-Latry's controls were people with a reimbursed prescription in July 2003, not a random sample. There were only 56 cases. — [PubMed 17513091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17513091/)
- Layton is restricted to people with a heat-related hospitalization and chronic conditions (SCCS), which limits generalizability. — [PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0243665)

### Inferences
- Exposure misclassification from dispensing records, not knowing whether drugs were actually taken during the heat, and dose-blind exposure probably bias estimates toward the null. Selection of healthier users (for example hypnotics in France) can produce spurious protective associations.
- Sex-stratified medication × heat estimates were absent in every abstract retrieved.

### Gaps
- No sex-stratified or age-stratified medication-heat effect estimates were retrieved.
