How we used the graph to connect medicines to heat

Almost no study asks "what does this medicine do in a heat wave?" System only holds a handful of those direct findings. But it holds thousands of findings on the halves of that question. So we built a bridge out of them.

medicine→body effect←heat

If a medicine weakens a body effect and heat strains the same effect, the two risks meet there. Each half is well studied. The whole is the connection the graph lets us draw.

Move 1

The bridge: two well-studied halves make one connection

We pulled every System finding that names one of our 15 medicine groups, heat, or one of five body effects (kidney injury, low sodium, dehydration, sweating, body temperature): 15,001 findings over 1,902 API calls. Then we kept the findings that link a medicine to an effect, and heat to an effect, and joined them on the effect.

Medicine never studied directly with heat in System Medicine also has a direct heat study Heat → effect Line width = number of studies
Blood pressure is left out on purpose. Most medicine → blood-pressure findings are trials of a blood-pressure drug doing its job, so they would swamp the picture without saying anything about heat. They are kept in the data (system_api_pathways.csv).
Move 2

Reading across: where the bridge is strong and where it is missing

The same pathways as numbers. Each cell counts System studies linking the medicine group to that effect. The heat row under each effect is the other half. The last column is the direct medicine × heat evidence the bridge stands in for.

The gaps are findings too. The medicines that stop sweating or raise body heat (anticholinergics, topiramate, stimulants) have 0–2 studies on those effects in System, even though classic heat advice worries about them most. Cool-Aid labels those claims "Early signs" instead of overstating them.

Move 3

Checking the bridge against what we already knew

A method that connects anything to anything is useless. So before trusting the bridge, we ran it on medicines whose heat risk is already in the literature and guidance, to see whether the graph finds them without being told.

13 of 15of our known heat-risk medicine groups reach heat through at least one effect other than blood pressure.
2 missedTopiramate/zonisamide works through sweating, which has almost no medicine studies in System. Epinephrine's risk is the pen spoiling in heat, a storage problem with no body effect to connect.
#1 of 19NSAIDs, which were not on our list, top the non-blood-pressure pathway score across every group we scored (kidney injury: 45 studies). Heat guidance already warns about them in the "triple whammy" with diuretics and ACE inhibitors.

Sources: system_api_pathway_drug_summary.csv (19 groups: our 15 plus 4 comparators), system_api_pathways.csv.

Move 4

From the graph to one person's medicine list

A clinician doesn't treat a medicine group, they treat a patient on three medicines at once. So we run the bridge per patient: for each body effect, which of their medicines weaken it, and does System have findings on both halves? When two or more medicines land on the same effect, the risks stack, and the clinician sees why.

Move 5

Pointing past our list

Run the bridge backwards and the graph suggests medicines we never looked at. These are the outpatient drugs with the most studies on the non-blood-pressure effects that heat also strains. They are leads, not findings.

Why these stay unvalidated. The graph links a medicine to an effect whatever the direction. Tolvaptan is here because it treats low sodium, not because it causes it. That is exactly why a person has to read the studies before any lead reaches a label. 262 candidates sit in system_hidden_drug_candidates.csv, marked unvalidated. Broad classes such as "antibiotic" are left out of this chart.
Move 6

System as a second reader, and us as its auditor

We extracted every number we quote from paper abstracts ourselves. System extracts findings from papers too, so we compared paper by paper: same number, same meaning, same direction. The connection runs both ways. Where System was wrong, we report it back.
